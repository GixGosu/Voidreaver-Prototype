# Space synth music sourcing sheet
Date: 2026-10-31   Brief: Dark synthwave / cold space electronic tracks that could loop under Voidreaver's boarding combat, flight run and menus.

Scope notes on method: I fetched each item page below with a web fetch tool. I cannot listen to audio, so "what it is" comes from the page's own text and track titles, not from my ears. The human must audition everything. OpenGameArt blocked all fetches (HTTP 403) and incompetech's track pages are JavaScript-rendered, so those items are listed as Unverified at the bottom rather than in the main groups.

## Questions this sheet answers
- Which tracks or packs with a stated commercial-use license fit a dark, driving synth tone for a browser game?
- Which of them are real loops (versus full songs that need an edit), and in what format?
- What attribution would the team have to ship for each?
- Which comparable games and albums show the target tone, and what should Voidreaver copy from them?

## Reference material (study only)

### Hotline Miami (2012) soundtrack — M.O.O.N., Perturbator, Scattle, Sun Araw, Coconuts and others
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotline_Miami
Shows: A 22-track soundtrack spanning electronic, techno, vaporwave and synthwave. M.O.O.N.'s "Hydrogen" is bass and drums led and was credited with boosting player focus. At the end of each level the music drops out to ambience while the player walks out past the bodies.
Lesson: Listen for how a constant, relentless beat keeps energy up across restarts, and how silence or ambience after the clear gives the reward. For Voidreaver, pair a driving loop in combat with a quiet bed during the room-clear and door-open moment.

### Hotline Miami 2: Wrong Number soundtrack — Carpenter Brut ("Roller Mobster", "Le Perv"), M.O.O.N., Castanets
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotline_Miami_2:_Wrong_Number
Shows: About fifty tracks chosen from roughly 200 submissions. "Roller Mobster" was used in the "Death Wish" chapter. "You Are the Blood" (Castanets) was used in the ending cutscene.
Lesson: Listen to how different tracks give each level a different identity. Voidreaver has 5 rooms and a flight run, so consider one distinct loop per room type rather than one track for everything. Also note the contrast between a loud synth track and a sparse ending piece.

### Carpenter Brut, "Trilogy" (2015)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter_Brut
Shows: The page classes it as darksynth, French electro, Italo disco revival and progressive metal; influences include John Carpenter and film scores. Hueso describes the music as mixing horror-film sounds with metal, rock and electronic.
Lesson: This is the reference for "violent, cinematic, and fun" (the page's words). Study how a heavy kick and a sawtooth bass line hold the rhythm while lead melodies stay sparse enough that gunfire SFX can cut through.

### Perturbator, "Dangerous Days" (2014) and "The Uncanny Valley" (2016)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbator
Shows: Synthwave blended with industrial, darksynth and gothic rock; cyberpunk and sci-fi influences (Akira, Ghost in the Shell); software emulations of the OB-X and CS-80. He contributed "Miami Disco" to Hotline Miami.
Lesson: The closest match to "dark synthwave with a sci-fi setting". Listen for the pad textures (CS-80 style brass pads) that give a "space" feel while the drums stay straight. Instrument choice, not tempo, is what makes it read as spaceship rather than neon city.

### FTL: Faster Than Light — Ben Prunty
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light
Shows: Each song has a calm "Explore" version and a more engaging "Battle" version built on it; both play at once and the game cross-fades between them depending on what is on screen. Nominated for IGN's Best Overall Music and Best PC Sound of 2012.
Lesson: The model to copy if the team ever wants intensity layers. Tim Beek's "no melody" and "light" variants (above) are the same idea as a purchased asset. Per CLAUDE.md scope this stays a future idea.

### Mozilla audio codec guide (technical reference for the format advice)
Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/Audio_codecs
Shows: Vorbis plays in Chrome and Firefox but not Safari. MP3 plays in Chrome, Firefox (since 71 without platform libraries) and Safari. The guide lists minimum latency of at least 100 ms for both MP3 and Vorbis, and says Opus has the lowest latency. It gives no gapless-looping guarantee for either.
Lesson: Gapless looping is a real risk for both codecs; test the seam in the actual browser.

## Direction notes
Notes below marked (engineering) are my own judgment and were not read from a page.

- **Short loops in the 120-130 BPM range for combat** (engineering). At 120 BPM a beat is 0.5 s and a 4/4 bar is 2 s, so a 2-5 s fight spans 1-2.5 bars; at 128 BPM a bar is 1.875 s. A range of roughly 115-135 BPM keeps the kick landing every half second, which matches the pump delay (0.45 s) and combo window (1.5 s) in the spec. Brain Dance and Mesmerizing Galaxy (124 BPM, unverified license) sit right in this range. Risk: slower ambient packs will feel disconnected from the fights; faster than ~140 BPM will blur against shotgun SFX.
- **Keep the music running across death and restart; do not restart the track** (engineering). The spec restarts a room in under 0.5 s and the player will die often. Restarting a track at bar one every 0.4 s freeze would sound like a glitch. Instead let the loop run on a continuous clock; on death, at most duck it (low-pass or -6 dB) during the 0.4 s freeze and restore it on reset, with the death stinger coming from the blips. Reset the track only on scene changes (flight to boarding, menu to game). Risk: a long track with no loop point feels repetitive after 20 deaths in one room; prefer a 60-90 s loop to a 16 s one. The 16 s ATLAS loop would grate.
- **One loop per room type plus a quiet clear bed** (from Hotline Miami's end-of-level ambience). Combat loop in rooms, a sparse pad (Serat or Sandro-style) once the door opens. Risk: crossfading needs a WebAudio GainNode graph, which is a code change for proto-architect, not something this sheet implements.
- **Loop length vs file size over file://** (engineering). Stereo WAV is about 10 MB per minute (NEFERA's 90 s WAV files are 12-15 MB, consistent with this). At 128-160 kbps OGG, a 90 s loop is roughly 1.5-2.5 MB. For a prototype of 7 tracks that is about 15 MB of audio, fine. A single 3:16 Sandro track at 320 kbps is about 7.5 MB, which is excessive for a loop. Risk: Chrome blocks `fetch` and XHR on `file://`, so `decodeAudioData` on loaded files will not work from a double-clicked `index.html`; the options are an `<audio>` element with `loop` (which has audible gaps in some browsers), or base64-embedded data in a classic script (about 33% larger). This is an architecture decision for proto-architect.
- **Format: ship OGG Vorbis, optionally with an MP3 fallback** (MDN above). Chrome and Firefox both play Vorbis; the target is desktop Chrome/Firefox only. MP3 works everywhere but MDN lists 100 ms or more minimum latency and MP3 encoder padding commonly leaves a gap at the loop point; Vorbis generally loops cleaner but MDN does not guarantee either. The human converts and trims loops; this agent does not. Risk: neither is gapless-guaranteed, so choose loops with a reverb-tail-free cut on a bar line and test the seam in both browsers.
- **Cheapest path to a first listen:** audition NEFERA (free, 3 moods), kontinuum (free, 9 loops), Tim Beek (about $7.50-$15, formal license, OGG) and Serat on FMA for menus, before spending on anything else. Risk: none of these is confirmed dark-industrial; they may sound brighter than Perturbator.
